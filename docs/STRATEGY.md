# Build something people choose to use

The core goal is a useful Brazilian alternative to platforms whose operators and incentives the community cannot control. P2P, Matrix, local storage, and data centers are implementation tools. The product must stand on its usefulness, reliability, and governance.

## First community

Recruit one existing group of 50–100 people around a recurring activity: a neighborhood association, university society, cooperative, or a community the founder already participates in. Start with 5–10 technical testers before inviting that group. Until the audience is chosen, assume a voluntary existing community; do not design acquisition around a generic national launch.

Move one activity people already need: event coordination, useful local announcements, mutual help, or a regular discussion. Preserve an easy way to reach members who have not joined. Test whether members choose to return without requiring them to learn network architecture.

The opening experience should answer three questions: What can I do here? Who is already here? How do I join this group? The eventual pilot must offer an ordinary invitation link and fast onboarding. Manual offer/answer pairing is a transport experiment, not the intended onboarding experience.

## Product promises

- Conversations follow time, with clear user-controlled subscriptions and notification settings.
- No advertising or attention ranking in the initial product. Any future recommendation feature must be explicit and controllable.
- Portuguese first, accessible interfaces, small payloads, and testing on inexpensive Android phones and weak connections.
- Exportable data, an explicit license, public specifications, and the ability for independent Brazilian operators to run supporting infrastructure.
- Plain explanations of who receives a conversation, where copies may remain, who governs a community, and how people can leave or recover access.

## Sovereignty in practice

Brazilian operation, maintainable open code, published protocols, locally controlled keys, transparent decision-making, and the ability to change providers are concrete forms of independence. Avoid substituting one indispensable private gatekeeper for another. Depending on an open standard is different from surrendering account access or product decisions to a foreign platform.

Default services must be explicit. If bootstrap, mailbox, TURN, storage, or notification servers are needed, support independently operated alternatives and document what each can observe. Brazilian hosting alone does not prevent influence through product incentives or opaque moderation. Governance and business incentives need attention too.

## Choose the transport using user evidence

Run the P2P proof and test a Brazilian-operated Matrix service as two implementation paths behind the same interface. The user experience should eventually hide routine network setup while retaining meaningful control.

Keep P2P when it improves availability, ownership, local connectivity, or operating cost. Add optional infrastructure when it improves discovery, offline delivery, or phone usability. Do not require every user to remain online or manually pair with every other member. A hybrid design must still explain which messages go through relays and where copies are stored.

For MSG Gov, shared use of Matrix is evidence of a possible protocol relationship, not permission or guaranteed interoperability. Integration requires authorized access, supported interfaces, federation policy, and compatible encryption. Keep it separate from the public product's first adoption milestone.

## What to measure before growing

Use interviews and small, consent-based measurements before introducing analytics infrastructure:

- Time from invitation to the first successfully received message.
- Why people return during the group's recurring activity and why they stop.
- Delivery delay, reconnect failures, identity recovery success, and support requests.
- Phone battery impact, download size, browser compatibility, and weak-network behavior.
- Cost and bandwidth per active community, including media and relay usage.

Review with the community weekly. Set pilot targets after observing the first testers; do not present invented retention percentages as success criteria.

Million-user planning begins with bounded replication, independently operated services, explicit protocol versions, and measurable bottlenecks. It does not require a data-center purchase or a nationwide architecture before the first community works.
